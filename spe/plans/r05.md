## The interesting prediction

The headline question: **does a topological edge state survive the noise of a real 2026 quantum computer?**

Polyacetylene is the plastic whose conductivity won the 2000 Chemistry Nobel. Its chain of alternating short and long bonds is the SSH model. When the chain ends on a weak bond, an electron placed at the end stays there. Topology protects that state. When the chain ends on a strong bond, the electron leaves. Noise does not respect topology, so it may wash out the difference.

Pre-registered physics prediction for real Garnet (8 sites, t = 2, 1000 shots, oracle circuit with 42 CZ):

| Quantity | Topological | Trivial | Ideal (topo, triv) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw edge probability | 0.60 ± 0.08 | 0.15 ± 0.06 | 0.767, 0.142 |
| Edge probability after keeping only one-excitation shots | 0.72 ± 0.07 | 0.15 ± 0.06 | same as ideal |
| Share of shots with exactly one excitation | 0.70 ± 0.10 | 0.70 ± 0.10 | 1.00 |
| Ratio of raw edge probabilities, topo over triv | 3 or more | | 5.4 |

Headline call: the edge state stays clearly visible on real hardware (ratio 3 or more on raw counts).

Baselines for the SPE "closer" check:
- **IQM's noise model.** `IQMFakeGarnet` run on the oracle circuit, computed after the prediction is committed and before the real probe. The question becomes: is Claude's intuition or IQM's own noise model closer to the real chip?
- **No noise.** The ideal values.
