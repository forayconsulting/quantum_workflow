# SPE round 6: a 54-qubit magnet on real IQM Emerald

## Context

The user wants harder problems that suit today's quantum computers, run for real. Rounds 1 to 5 used at most 10 qubits, and a laptop could check every result in a second. Round 6 moves to the whole 54-qubit Emerald chip. The model is the kicked Ising magnet that IBM used in its 2023 "utility" paper (Kim et al., Nature 618). That paper started a public argument. Classical groups reproduced its numbers within weeks by tracking only the qubits that can affect each measured one. This round asks the same question on our own hardware: does a 54-qubit, 255-CZ circuit on Emerald still carry the right physics, and do the agents report honestly that a laptop can check it?

## The experiment

One step of the kicked Ising magnet is: rotate every qubit by RX(θ), then apply RZZ(−π/2) on all 85 couplers in 4 layers. RZZ(−π/2) equals CZ followed by S† on both qubits, so one step costs 85 CZ. Start in all zeros. Run 3 steps (255 CZ). Measure all 54 qubits. Report the mean magnetization ⟨Z⟩ over the 54 qubits.

One job holds 4 circuits at 250 shots each: θ = 0, 0.2π, 0.35π, 0.5π. At θ = 0 the ideal answer is exactly 1, so that circuit measures the noise alone. Dividing the other values by it is the simplest error mitigation (rescaling).

Why a laptop can still check it: the full state of 54 qubits needs 2^54 numbers, which no laptop holds. But the Z value of one qubit after 3 steps depends only on qubits at most 2 couplers away, at most 13 qubits. The grader computes each qubit's exact value from that small patch. This is the same trick that classical groups used against the IBM paper.

## The prediction

Headline call: **Emerald keeps the physics.** After rescaling by the θ = 0 circuit, the magnetization at θ = 0.2π and 0.35π lands within 0.10 of the exact value.

| Quantity (mean over 54 qubits, depth 3) | Predicted | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Raw ⟨Z⟩ at θ = 0 | 0.92 ± 0.04 | The state stays all zeros, CZ on zeros does almost nothing, so readout error (about 2 to 3 percent per qubit) dominates |
| Raw over exact, θ = 0.2π and 0.35π | 0.75 ± 0.12 | Each qubit sits in about 11 CZs of 99.5 percent fidelity plus readout |
| Rescaled minus exact, θ = 0.2π and 0.35π | within ±0.10 | Rescaling removes readout and most CZ decay. It over-corrects slightly because entangled states decay faster than all zeros |
| Raw ⟨Z⟩ at θ = 0.5π | within 0.10 of exact | Ideal is near 0 at the Clifford point, and noise pulls toward 0 too |
| Billed seconds per 4 × 250-shot job | 1.0 to 2.0 | Garnet billed 1.0 for 2 × 1000 shots |

Baselines for the closer check, computed after the prediction is committed and before the probe:
- **Calibration product.** Per qubit: exact value times the product of IRB CZ fidelities inside its patch, times (1 − 2 × readout error). Built from Emerald's live calibration.
- **No noise.** The exact values.

## Risks

- Emerald may bill far more than 1 second. Gate 6 shrinks the real lane, and the probe carries the physics.
- Starter access to Emerald may be limited. The probe finds out first.
- Agents may transpile the circuit with extra layers, so patches grow past 22 qubits. That fails fairly, since the task fixes 3 steps.
- Emerald may recalibrate during the run (F11). Every job records its calibration set.
